By any measure, 2008 was a terrible year for the American economy. The financial system came close to seizing in the autumn, and it was only through extraordinary actions by the Federal Reserve and the Treasury that a far worse outcome was averted. Losses at financial institutions were vast; confidence was eroded; and the real economy contracted sharply in the fourth quarter. Our own book value declined meaningfully, driven principally by mark-to-market losses on equity holdings and derivatives. We remain confident in the long-term prospects of our operating businesses and in the underwriting discipline of our insurance subsidiaries.
