Our relative performance in 1999 was the worst in my tenure. The prices commanded for the kinds of businesses we understand have risen to levels at which the arithmetic of future returns has become unattractive, and we have been unwilling to stretch. We see enormous amounts of capital flowing toward enterprises whose economics we struggle to model, and whose long-term competitive advantages we cannot confidently describe. We continue to prefer understanding to excitement.
